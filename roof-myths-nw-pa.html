5 Roof Myths NW Pennsylvania Homeowners Still Believe.

At the door, the same five sentences come up every week. Each one sounds reasonable. Each one keeps people from finding out what the 2026 storms did to their house.

Published November 17, 2026 by Bret Ruth, TableTurn Digital. General information only. It does not diagnose a specific home or determine insurance coverage.

Myth 1: “My roof doesn't leak, so it's fine.”

Hail bruises shingles and knocks the protective granules off. That damage rarely leaks the first year. It wears through slowly and shows up as a leak years later, usually long after anyone connects it to the storm.

Myth 2: “If there was hail damage, I'd see it from the ground.”

From the yard, a bruised shingle looks like every other shingle. Most hits only show up close, on the shingles, the vents and the gutters. That's why an inspection means getting on the roof and taking photos.

Myth 3: “Damage only counts if it's on the roof.”

Hail dents gutters and downspouts, cracks vinyl siding and tears window screens. A good inspection looks at all of it. We're partnered directly with The Exterior Company (TEC), which does roofs, gutters and siding, so we check the whole outside.

Myth 4: “It's too late in the year to check.”

Any dry day works, even in November and December. It's better to know what you're dealing with before snow and ice sit on the roof all winter.

Myth 5: “My insurance company will tell me if there's damage.”

Your insurer doesn't come look unless you call. Our job is to document what we find with clear photos. Your insurer decides coverage.

Common questions

Can a roof have hail damage without leaking?

Yes. Bruised shingles and lost granules usually don't leak right away. The damage tends to show up as leaks later, as the shingle wears through.

Can I check for hail damage from the ground?

You can spot some clues, like dented gutters, downspouts and siding. Most hits on shingles only show up close, which is why an inspection includes the roof itself.

Is it too late in the year for a roof inspection?

No. Any dry day works. It's better to know before snow and ice sit on the roof all winter.

Does the inspection decide my insurance claim?

No. We document what we find. Your insurance company decides what your policy covers.
