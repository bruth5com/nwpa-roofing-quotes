Ice Dams and Winter Roof Leaks in NW Pennsylvania: What to Look For.

The snow is here. If you see a ridge of ice along your roof edge this winter, here's what it means, what to look for from the ground, and what not to do.

Published November 24, 2026 by Bret Ruth, TableTurn Digital. General information only. It does not diagnose a specific home or determine insurance coverage.

What an ice dam is

An ice dam is a ridge of ice that builds up along the lower edge of a roof, usually right over the gutter. Heat from the house warms the upper part of the roof and melts the snow there. The water runs down to the edge, where the roof is colder, and freezes again. Each melt adds to the ridge.

Once the ridge is big enough, the next melt has nowhere to drain. Water can pool behind the ice and work its way under the shingles, which is how a roof that never leaked in the summer starts dripping in January.

Four signs you can check from the ground

  1. A thick band of ice along the roof edge. Not just a few icicles, but a solid ridge sitting over the gutter.
  2. Big icicles hanging from the gutters. Heavy ice can bend gutters and pull the hangers loose.
  3. Stains or peeling paint inside. Check ceilings and the tops of walls near the outside walls, right under the roof edge.
  4. Gutters sagging or pulling away. Look along the whole run, especially at the corners.

What not to do

Don't climb a ladder in snow and ice. Winter falls are the real danger here, not the ice. Don't chip the ice with a hammer, shovel or ax. It's easy to crack shingles and damage the gutter, and that turns one problem into two.

What to do instead

Take photos from the ground of the ice, the icicles and the gutters, and of any stains inside, and note the date. If water is coming inside, catch the drips, move anything that can get damaged, and call a roofing professional.

Ice dams are often a sign that heat is escaping into the attic or that the roof edge needs attention. That's worth a look once the roof is clear.

Common questions

What causes ice dams?

Heat from the house melts snow on the upper roof. The water runs down and freezes again at the colder roof edge, building a ridge of ice that can trap the next melt.

Are icicles the same as an ice dam?

Not always. A few icicles are common. A thick, solid ridge of ice along the roof edge is the sign of an ice dam.

Should I chip the ice off my roof?

No. Chipping ice can crack shingles and damage gutters, and climbing a ladder in winter is dangerous. Photograph it from the ground and call a professional if water comes inside.
