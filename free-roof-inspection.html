What Gets Checked

  • Shingles: hail hits, bruising, cracks, lifted or missing tabs, heavy granule loss
  • Metal panels: dents, loose fasteners and seam problems
  • Flashing around chimneys, walls, skylights and valleys
  • Pipe boots, vents and exhaust caps
  • Gutters and downspouts, including dents and granules collecting inside
  • Siding and window trim on the side that faced the storm
  • Signs of leaks inside, like ceiling stains or damp attic wood, if you'd like me to look

About Insurance

Pennsylvania homeowners policies often cover sudden storm damage like wind and hail, but not normal wear or age. Photos taken soon after a storm give you a clear record to share with your insurance company.

Please note: I document what I find. I'm not an insurance company or a public adjuster, and I don't negotiate claims for you. Your insurance company decides what is covered under your policy.

Common Questions

Is the roof inspection really free?

Yes. Homeowners never pay for the inspection. If you later hire a contractor I recommend, that contractor pays TableTurn Digital a referral fee. You're never required to hire anyone.

Who decides which contractor I'm sent to?

I recommend the crew that fits your roof, your situation and your budget, and I explain why. Insurance related shingle, siding and gutter work usually goes to an established storm restoration company. Metal roofs, barns, outbuildings and cash repairs often go to an experienced local crew, including Amish roofing crews. The final choice is always yours.

Will you handle my insurance claim?

No. I photograph and document what I find so you have a clear record. I'm not an insurance company or a public adjuster and don't negotiate claims. Your insurance company decides coverage under your policy.

What if there's no damage?

Then I'll tell you so. You get the photos and an honest answer, and nobody will push you into work you don't need.

Should I climb up and look myself first?

Please don't. Wet, steep or damaged roofs are dangerous. Look from the ground, check your gutters for granules, and let a professional handle the rest.
