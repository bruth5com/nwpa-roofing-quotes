What Does Hail Damage Look Like on a Roof?

Learn the visible clues on asphalt shingles, metal roofing, gutters and siding, and what you can safely document without climbing onto your roof.

By Bret Ruth, TableTurn Digital, Franklin PA · Published October 22, 2026 · General homeowner information, not a diagnosis of any specific roof

About the pictures: The four example images on this page are AI generated illustrations, labeled as such, to show what to look for. They are not photos of real homes or real inspections.

The short answer

Possible hail damage on asphalt shingles may appear as random dark impact marks, displaced granules or bruised areas. Metal roofing and softer metal components may show scattered dents. Gutters, downspouts, siding and window screens can provide safer ground level clues, but a photograph from the yard cannot confirm the roof's condition.

What hail damage can look like on asphalt shingles

Were you on the April 15, 2026 storm path? The National Weather Service logged hail up to golf ball size from Greenville through Sandy Lake, Polk, Franklin and Cranberry. See the storm map and every official report.

Hail impacts are often random rather than arranged in straight lines. A close professional inspection may find displaced granules, a dark exposed area, cracking or bruising in the shingle mat. The exact appearance changes with the shingle's age, color, condition, hail size and wind.

Do not perform a “bruise test” yourself. Walking on a wet, steep or damaged roof is hazardous and may create additional damage.

What hail damage can look like on a metal roof

Metal panels may show shallow, scattered dimples or deeper dents after hail. Marks are often easier to see when light crosses the panel at an angle. Cosmetic denting and functional damage are not automatically the same; seams, fasteners, coatings and water shedding performance must also be evaluated.

Ground level clues homeowners can check safely

You may not be able to see shingle bruising from the driveway. Start with accessible exterior surfaces and photograph what you find from a safe distance.

  • Dents on gutters or downspouts
  • Fresh roofing granules near downspout outlets
  • Cracks or impact marks on siding
  • Torn window screens
  • Dents on roof vents or flashing visible from the ground
  • Fresh water stains or leaks inside the home

Keep away from downed wires, damaged trees and unstable building materials. Contact the utility or emergency services when an electrical or immediate life safety hazard exists.

How to document possible hail damage safely

  1. Write down the date and approximate time of the storm.
  2. Take wide photographs showing each side of the property.
  3. Take closer photographs of accessible gutters, downspouts, siding and debris.
  4. Photograph interior leaks or stains without disturbing wet electrical areas.
  5. Save relevant local weather alerts or reports.
  6. Do not climb onto the roof; request a qualified inspection.

What a professional roof inspection should document

A useful inspection record should identify the roofing material, approximate age and condition; photograph each roof slope and relevant accessories; distinguish observed impact marks from ordinary wear; note active leaks or emergency needs; and explain whether repair, monitoring or further evaluation is appropriate.

Ask who is performing the inspection, whether the contractor carries appropriate insurance and Pennsylvania registration where required, and whether you will receive photographs and written findings.

Hail damage and homeowners insurance

A roofing contractor can document observable conditions and provide repair information. The insurance carrier decides whether a loss is covered under the policy. No photograph, inspection or contractor statement guarantees claim approval, payment amount or roof replacement.

Review your own policy and deadlines, keep copies of communications and avoid anyone who promises a guaranteed outcome or offers to misrepresent wear as storm damage.

Frequently asked questions

Can I see hail damage from the ground?

Sometimes you can see supporting clues, but shingle bruising is often difficult to confirm from the yard. Ground level photographs are a safe starting point, not a diagnosis.

Should I climb onto my roof after a hailstorm?

No. Wet or damaged roofing can be unsafe. Arrange an inspection with a qualified professional.

Does a dent mean I need a new roof?

Not automatically. Material, age, location and severity all matter. Cosmetic and functional damage should be distinguished.

Does hail damage guarantee insurance coverage?

No. Coverage is determined by your policy and insurer. A contractor can document conditions but cannot promise approval.
