The April 15 Hail Storm: Franklin, Polk, Cranberry and Sandy Lake

On the evening of April 15, a single storm dropped hail from Greenville to Cranberry, with golf ball size stones in three places. If your home sits on that line and nobody has looked at your roof, now is the time, before snow covers it.

What Happened

Around 6:15 PM on Wednesday, April 15, 2026, a long track thunderstorm crossed from Ohio into Mercer County and kept going east into Venango County until about 7:45 PM. The National Weather Service office in Pittsburgh logged 14 hail reports along its path. Most were quarter to half dollar size. Three were golf ball size, and one report south of Cranberry described stones close to 2 inches and damage to numerous cars.

Hail that size can bruise shingles, knock off the protective granules and dent gutters, vents and siding. That kind of damage is easy to miss from the yard, and it often doesn't leak until a winter of freezing and thawing works on it.

Signs Your Home Took Hail

You don't need to climb anything. From the ground, look for:

  • Dents in gutters, downspouts, window wraps or the metal vents on the roof
  • Dark granules collected at the ends of downspouts or inside the gutters
  • Cracked or chipped vinyl siding on the side that faced the storm, usually west
  • Dings on a car, grill, shed or mailbox that was outside that evening
  • New ceiling stains or damp spots in the attic after a heavy rain

If you see any of these, the roof probably took hits too. See what a free exterior inspection checks for more.

Why Get It Checked Before Winter

Snow will cover your roof for weeks at a time, and damage that can't be seen can't be photographed. Bruised shingles also take on water during freeze and thaw cycles, and a small problem in October can be a ceiling leak in February.

Common Questions

It's been months. Is it too late?

Hail damage doesn't go away on its own, and it's much easier to photograph now than under snow. Many policies have time limits for reporting damage, so check yours. We document what's there. Your insurance company decides coverage.

My roof looks fine from the ground. Could it still be damaged?

Yes. Hail bruises shingles and strips granules in ways you can't see from the yard. Dented gutters or a dinged car from the same storm are good signs the roof took hits too.

Should I climb up and look myself?

Please don't. Fall roofs are slick with leaves and frost, and walking on hail damaged shingles can make it worse. Look from the ground and let us handle the rest.
