Hail Hits More Than the Roof: 5 Spots to Check From the Yard.

Most people think of shingles when they hear hail, but the same storm also hits everything else on the outside of your house. Those other spots are often easier to check, and you can do it all with your feet on the ground.

Published November 3, 2026 by Bret Ruth, TableTurn Digital. General information only. It does not diagnose a specific home or determine insurance coverage.

1. Gutters

Walk along the house and look at the front edge of the gutters. Small round dents are the usual sign. A low morning or evening sun makes them easier to see, because the dents throw small shadows.

2. Downspouts

Check the metal for dents, then look at the bottom where the water comes out. A pile of dark grit can be granules washed off the shingles. Some granule loss is normal on an older roof, so treat it as a clue and not a verdict.

3. Siding

Look for chips, cracks or dents, mostly on the side of the house that faced the storm. Wind drives hail sideways, so one wall can take most of it while the others look untouched.

4. Window screens

Screens are thin, so they can show small tears or dings in the metal frames. They are also easy to check up close from the ground floor.

5. Roof vents

Metal roof vents are soft and tend to show hits. Look at them from the yard, with binoculars or your phone's zoom if that helps. Please don't climb onto the roof.
