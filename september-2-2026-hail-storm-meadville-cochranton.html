The September 2 Storm: Meadville and Cochranton

Late on the afternoon of September 2, a storm dropped half dollar hail just east of Meadville and quarter hail near Cochranton, and took trees down along the way. If your home was under it and nobody has looked at your roof, get it checked before snow covers it.

What Happened

Around 5:00 PM on Wednesday, September 2, 2026, a thunderstorm moved southeast across Crawford County. The National Weather Service logged 1.25 inch hail (half dollar size) about 3 miles east of Meadville at 5:02 PM and 1 inch hail (quarter size) about 1 mile north of Cochranton at 6:05 PM. Trees came down southeast and southwest of Meadville and northwest of Cochranton, and the storm kept going into Venango County north of Oil City.

Hail that size can bruise shingles, knock off the protective granules and dent gutters, vents and siding. It's easy to miss from the yard, and it usually doesn't leak until a winter of freezing and thawing works on it.

Crawford County Is in an SBA Disaster Area

The U.S. Small Business Administration declared a disaster for severe storms and flash flooding from September 2 through 3, 2026 (declaration PA-20044). Erie and Clinton are the primary counties, and Crawford County is included as a neighboring county. Homeowners there can apply for low interest SBA disaster loans to repair physical damage until November 23, 2026.

Please note: this is an SBA loan program, not a FEMA grant or insurance coverage, and it covers storm and flood damage generally. Learn more and apply at sba.gov. Our inspection photos can help you document damage for any claim or application.

Signs Your Home Took Hail or Wind

You don't need to climb anything. From the ground, look for:

  • Dents in gutters, downspouts, window wraps or the metal vents on the roof
  • Dark granules collected at the ends of downspouts or inside the gutters
  • Missing, lifted or creased shingles, especially on the side that faced the storm
  • Dings on a car, grill, shed or mailbox that was outside that evening
  • New ceiling stains or damp spots in the attic after a heavy rain

Why Get It Checked Before Winter

Snow will cover your roof for weeks at a time, and damage that can't be seen can't be photographed. Bruised or lifted shingles also take on water during freeze and thaw cycles, and a small problem in October can be a ceiling leak in February.

Common Questions

The hail was only half dollar size. Is that enough to cause damage?

It can be, especially on older shingles, gutters, vents and siding. Wind from the same storm can also lift shingles. The only way to know is to have someone look. The inspection is free.

Does the SBA disaster declaration mean insurance will pay?

No. The SBA declaration opens low interest disaster loans for Crawford County homeowners until November 23, 2026. Insurance coverage is separate, and your insurance company decides what your policy covers.

Is the inspection really free?

Yes. No cost and no obligation. You get photos of everything we find and a plain explanation either way.

Should I climb up and look myself?

Please don't. Fall roofs are slick with leaves and frost, and walking on damaged shingles can make it worse. Look from the ground and let us handle the rest.
